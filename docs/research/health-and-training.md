# Nutrition, training and family learning — Phase 1

Review date: 2026-10-04. Scope: an initial source-backed product-content review. This does not validate a personal calorie target, every exercise prescription or long-term safety of a restrictive diet.

## Decisions supported by this research

Food selection, meal timing and energy intake need independent controls. A 12-month trial found no significant weight-loss advantage from adding the tested eating window to calorie restriction; this should prevent a blanket “fasting is best” label. [H04: Calorie restriction with or without time-restricted eating](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35443107/)

OMAD is a recorded owner preference. The located controlled studies were limited in duration and protocol; one-meal glucose findings were not uniformly favorable. Provide an evidence card and suitability review, with editable alternatives. Do not automatically convert an OMAD preference into a restrictive personalized plan. [H05: Reduced meal frequency without caloric restriction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17413096/); [H06: Reduced meal frequency and glucose regulation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17998028/)

A general program should support appropriate nutrition, activity, behavior tracking and ongoing review. It should not promise a particular body shape by a date. [H01: Choosing a safe and successful weight-loss program](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/choosing-a-safe-successful-weight-loss-program)

## Required diet library

“Product treatment” is a proposed design decision. Evidence labels below describe this review's coverage, not a formal GRADE assessment.

| Approach | Evidence and limitation | Product treatment and practical review |
|---|---|---|
| Balanced eating | General agency guidance supports sustainable individualized eating and behavior change. [H01: Choosing a safe and successful weight-loss program](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/choosing-a-safe-successful-weight-loss-program) | Baseline flexible option; adapt food preferences, cost and preparation. |
| Mediterranean-style | A large trial reanalysis addresses cardiovascular events in older high-risk adults. Appearance/weight outcomes cannot be assumed for every user. [H09: Mediterranean diet primary cardiovascular prevention](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29897866/) | Supported comparison option; distinguish long-term health evidence from physique forecasting. |
| Higher protein | Controlled research investigates protein and lean-mass responses during weight loss; only indexed material was accessible here. [H12: High-protein diets during weight loss](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23739654/) | Review protein adequacy, personal suitability and food sources; select a quantitative target only after inputs and fuller formula/content review. |
| Vegetarian | Plant-focused options need nutrient planning; little/no animal food can affect B12 adequacy. [H11: Vitamin B12 consumer fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-Consumer/) | Distinguish inclusion of eggs/dairy, preferences and reliable nutrient sources. |
| Vegan | A small eight-week twin trial supports selected short-term cardiometabolic improvements, with limited generalizability. [H10: Vegan versus omnivorous diets in identical twins](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38032644/) | Offer as an option with nutrient planning; do not assume every vegan food pattern has the same quality. |
| Low carbohydrate | DIETFITS found no significant 12-month weight difference from healthy low-fat eating in its population. [H08: DIETFITS randomized clinical trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29466592/) | Optional preference; no requirement to cut carbs based solely on an appearance goal. |
| Ketogenic | A located trial evaluated a specific program in obesity/hyperlipidemia; it does not settle suitability for all users. [H13: Ketogenic versus low-fat diet trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15148063/) | Keep distinct from moderate low-carb eating; review restrictions, adequacy and medical context before suggestions. |
| Intermittent fasting / time-restricted eating | Timing approaches differ. The cited trial does not establish superiority; professional guidance identifies excluded populations and gaps. [H04: Calorie restriction with or without time-restricted eating](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35443107/); [H03: Intermittent fasting and type 2 diabetes](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/professionals/diabetes-discoveries-practice/patients-intermittent-fasting) | Optional timing layer, not an XP challenge. Evaluate medication/health context before personalization. |
| OMAD | Short controlled studies do not establish long-term universal safety or superiority. [H05: Reduced meal frequency without caloric restriction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17413096/); [H06: Reduced meal frequency and glucose regulation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17998028/) | Preserve the preference; show limits, evaluate whether adequate intake is feasible and allow alternatives without penalty. |
| Carnivore | A social-media survey is self-selected and uncontrolled despite favorable self-reports; lipid concerns were reported in a subset. [H07: Carnivore diet survey of 2029 adults](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34934897/) | Comparison and logging supported; no default recommendation or “proven optimal” label. Broader evidence review remains needed before any recommendation. |
| Clear liquids | Medical-purpose dietary instructions require provider supervision. [H14: Clear liquid diet](https://www.medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000205.htm) | Educational entry and clinician-directed logging, not an ordinary weight-loss plan generator. |
| Full liquids | Different from clear liquids; fiber/micronutrient limitations can remain. [H15: Full liquid diet](https://www.medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000206.htm) | Explain the medical context and distinguish from complete meal replacements. |
| Nutritionally complete meal replacements | NICE addresses supported low/very-low-energy clinical strategies with completeness and time limits. [H16: Physical activity and diet, NG246](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng246/chapter/Physical-activity-and-diet) | Do not treat a homemade shake or every commercial product as complete. Review the specific product/program and applicable clinical context. |
| Commercial liquid weight-loss programs | Evidence varies by actual formulation, support and protocol; the sources above cannot validate an unnamed program. | Keep product-specific evidence pending. Do not convert the category into an endorsement or recommend unsupervised very-low-energy intake. |

For every live library entry, Phase 5 must complete the method, evidence, adequacy, cost/adherence, training-fit, contraindication and review-date fields. A category's inclusion here is not completion of its production content.

## Training model

General adult guidance pairs aerobic activity with muscle-strengthening work at least twice weekly; this is context, not the owner's individualized starting volume. [H17: Adult activity overview](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html)

A primary resistance-training study tested high- and lower-repetition protocols in trained men, with sets to failure. It supports evaluating alternatives to exclusively heavy lifting, while its supervised protocol should not be copied automatically into a beginner routine. [H18: Resistance training load trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27174923/)

Proposed app behavior:
- Match full-body, upper/lower, push/pull/legs or five-day structures to actual availability and ability.
- Treat calisthenics skill practice, resistance work, treadmill walking, conditioning and recovery as shared workload.
- Track muscle groups, effort and recent sessions so adding boxing does not silently add a second intense conditioning block.
- Keep lighter resistance/high-repetition preference, existing equipment and exclusions in the profile.
- Gate sprint/HIIT/high-intensity choices by suitability and progressive preparation; exact protocols need later reviewed exercise specifications.
- Daily activity can include a recovery session. Morning routine preference does not imply hard training every morning.
- Collect actual exercise restrictions, starting ability, available loads and session duration before a personal plan.

## Boxing addition — confirmed scope

The owner selected home shadowboxing, footwork and fitness, with bag work if equipment is available. No sparring was requested. England Boxing offers coach-created home drills; these provide a candidate resource, not automatic assignment of that session to this user. [H19: Shadowboxing drills, week three](https://www.englandboxing.org/news_articles/england-boxing-shadow-boxing-drills-week-three/)

Proposed features: skill-focused session templates, configurable rounds/rest, technique notes, equipment checks and coordination with upper-body resistance work. Bag work requires confirmed equipment and appropriate introductory instruction. Progress is practice consistency and skill development, not impact force.

## Parent goal: help daughter become a cheer flyer

The owner's goal is to learn appropriate lifting/supporting skills. USA Cheer stresses ability-appropriate progressions and trained spotting; equipment and extra spotters do not replace skill readiness. [H20: National Cheer Safety Month](https://usacheer.org/safety/national-cheer-safety-month); [H21: Safety and risk management training](https://usacheer.org/safety/usa-cheer-safety-risk-management)

Proposed pathway:
1. Record the parent learning goal without opening a child account or inventing the daughter's age/ability.
2. Identify qualified coaching and the applicable discipline/level.
3. Schedule coached basing/spotting instruction and supervised practice.
4. Schedule only coach-approved home preparation.
5. Record coach-confirmed milestones and family time.

The app must not infer that a parent can safely perform an elevated lift from body weight, strength scores, a video watched or XP earned. A software checkbox documents an assessment; it cannot replace one.

## Measurements and personal suitability

Proposed data behavior: retain units, measurement date/method, estimates versus observations and editable assumptions. Use trends rather than a single weigh-in. NIDDK's model is a research lead for later forecasting-method review; the app has not implemented or validated that model. [H02: Body Weight Planner](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/body-weight-planner)

No personal calorie/macronutrient, electrolyte, fluid, training-volume or weight-loss timeline has been calculated. The profile still needs weight-unit confirmation, relevant health/medication/eating-history context, current training ability and scheduling constraints. These gaps affect personal prescription, not the ability to design the app.

The weight target and visible-abs goal remain separate desired outcomes. Photos are optional progress references, not precise body-fat measurements.
