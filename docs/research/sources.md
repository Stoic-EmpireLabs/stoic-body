# Phase 1 source register

Checked 2026-10-04. 37 sources. Dates describe publication/edition only where established; an unknown date remains unknown. The checked date is separate from the evidence's age.

Some PubMed pages blocked direct retrieval; indexed abstracts are explicitly identified. No clinical claim below relies on an invented full-text review. These sources support product-design decisions and later content review, not a personalized treatment plan.

## H03 — Intermittent fasting and type 2 diabetes

[Source](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/professionals/diabetes-discoveries-practice/patients-intermittent-fasting) — NIDDK. Expert interview. Publication/edition: not established in this pass. Access: page.

Supported finding: Fasting needs population-specific consideration; studies often exclude eating-disorder history and evidence gaps remain.

Limit: Expert discussion is not a universal OMAD safety assessment.

## H04 — Calorie restriction with or without time-restricted eating

[Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35443107/) — Liu et al., NEJM via PubMed. Primary randomized trial. Publication/edition: 2022-04-21. Access: search-indexed abstract.

Supported finding: In 139 adults with obesity, adding an eating window did not significantly improve 12-month weight outcomes over calorie restriction alone.

Limit: One specific eating window and study population; not a direct OMAD trial.

## H05 — Reduced meal frequency without caloric restriction

[Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17413096/) — Stote et al., AJCN via PubMed. Primary pilot crossover trial. Publication/edition: 2007-04. Access: search-indexed abstract.

Supported finding: Compared one versus three meals during two eight-week periods with weight-maintenance food intake.

Limit: Small pilot and short periods cannot establish long-term suitability for this owner.

## H06 — Reduced meal frequency and glucose regulation

[Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17998028/) — Carlson et al., Metabolism via PubMed. Primary crossover study. Publication/edition: 2007-12. Access: search-indexed abstract.

Supported finding: The one-meal condition produced less favorable morning glucose responses than three meals in the studied protocol.

Limit: Protocol/population specific; do not extrapolate to every fasting pattern.

## H07 — Carnivore diet survey of 2029 adults

[Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34934897/) — Lennerz et al., Current Developments in Nutrition via PubMed. Primary observational survey. Publication/edition: 2021-11-02. Access: search-indexed abstract.

Supported finding: Respondents reported satisfaction and perceived benefits; a lipid-reporting subset had elevated LDL.

Limit: Self-selected, self-reported, uncontrolled evidence; cannot establish causation or long-term safety.

## H08 — DIETFITS randomized clinical trial

[Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29466592/) — Gardner et al., JAMA via PubMed. Primary randomized trial. Publication/edition: 2018-02-20. Access: page.

Supported finding: Healthy low-fat and healthy low-carbohydrate groups did not differ significantly in 12-month weight change.

Limit: Does not prove all dietary methods equivalent or validate strict keto for everyone.

## H09 — Mediterranean diet primary cardiovascular prevention

[Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29897866/) — Estruch et al., NEJM via PubMed. Primary trial reanalysis. Publication/edition: 2018. Access: search-indexed abstract.

Supported finding: Reanalysis supports cardiovascular prevention benefits in the older, high-risk PREDIMED population.

Limit: Population and cardiovascular outcomes differ from a younger person's appearance goal.

## H10 — Vegan versus omnivorous diets in identical twins

[Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38032644/) — Landry et al., JAMA Network Open via PubMed. Primary randomized trial. Publication/edition: 2023-11. Access: search-indexed abstract.

Supported finding: An eight-week study of 22 twin pairs reported improvements in selected cardiometabolic measures on a healthy vegan diet.

Limit: Small, short trial with initial meal provision; not a long-term universal superiority result.

## H11 — Vitamin B12 consumer fact sheet

[Source](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-Consumer/) — NIH Office of Dietary Supplements. Official nutrition guidance. Publication/edition: not established in this pass. Access: search-indexed guidance.

## H12 — High-protein diets during weight loss

[Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23739654/) — Pasiakos et al. via PubMed. Primary randomized trial. Publication/edition: 2013. Access: search-indexed abstract.

Supported finding: Examines protein intake, fat-free mass and muscle protein synthesis during controlled weight loss.

Limit: Research lead supports reviewing protein adequacy; a personal gram target is not derived in this phase. Direct full-page retrieval failed in this research pass; only indexed abstract material was available.
